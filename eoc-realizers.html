EOC Three-Class Realizer Comparison

Accelerated map T(m) = (3m+1)/2a(m) on odd m. Each valuation word (a1,…,aN) is realized by one residue class mod 2SN+1; synthetic words are drawn from their least positive realizer r(D). Notation follows De Jesús, A Global Occupation Conjecture for the Accelerated 3x+1 Map, Rev. 7 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22906673): drift Rn = Sn − n·log₂3, occupation Oc = #{n : Rn ≤ c}, endpoint depth E(D) = SN − log₂ r(D).

    Selection-effect control: record holders vs ordinary orbits

    When D is read off the orbit of its own seed m₀, m₀ realizes D, so r(D) ≡ m₀ (mod 2SN+1) and r(D) ≤ m₀. Hence E(D)/SN ≥ 1 − log₂m₀/SN automatically, as De Jesús pointed out. The residual log₂(m₀/r(D)) is what is left after removing that effect. Ordinary orbits are non-record seeds matched to the record holders on word length N or valuation depth SN (selector above), drawn at random with a fixed seed. Seeds above the record search range are not checked for record status. For L₁ episodes, the control is the next odd seed above each record with the same lifetime L₁; it cannot be a record.

    Near-record control: matched on N and seed size

    Records are essentially unique at their exact (N, SN). Since SN = N·log₂3 + log₂m₀ + EN, fixing both pins the seed size to within the carry excess, and almost no other seed shares them. So this control relaxes the match. For each delay/glide record it takes the K non-record odd seeds m ≤ M with |N(m) − N| ≤ dN that are closest in κ = log₂m₀/N, the quantity that fixes the mean valuation (μ = log₂3 + κ + EN/N). Each statistic is fitted on the neighbours as a + b·(κ − κn) + c·(N(m) − N). The record's residual from a is reported in units of the neighbours' residual spread (z). Records are the κ-minima for their size, so the neighbours lie mostly on one side and the fit extrapolates slightly. μ is included as a check: after the adjustment its residual is only the carry-excess term EN/N. Excluded seeds are every odd seed ≤ M that sets a new odd-seed maximum of delay or glide, a superset of the record holders. Neighbouring orbits often merge with the record's, so the comparisons are not independent.

    Separated returns (EOC Open Problem F)

    The occupation Oc = #{n ≥ 0 : Rn ≤ c} splits into episodes, which are maximal runs inside the corridor: Oc = Σ ℓi. The first episode is the single window ℓ0 = Lc + 1. Everything after it, Oc − ℓ0, is carried by separated returns. That is the part Open Problem F asks to control. By the entry lemma (Prop. 13.1) every re-entry at time a has da−1 = 1 and 0 ≤ c − Ra < log₂(3/2). The charge ratio QK/Oc uses the single-window charge of Rem. 13.3: QK = Σ (1 + K(log₂mai + ci)), with c0 = c and ci = c − Rai. Whole orbits are counted past their arrival at 1, so Oc is the full-orbit occupation. Synthetic words are counted within the word only.

    Carry-walk residue tower

    Modulo q = pe the carry sum factors through the walk u0 = 1, uj+1 = uj·2dj·3−1 on Ke = ⟨2,3⟩ ≤ (ℤ/qℤ)×, since CN ≡ 3N−1·Σ uj. This section is an empirical Fourier diagnostic informed by the theorem of De Jesús, Exact Spectral Inheritance in Prime-Power Resolution Towers (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23088511), not a consequence of it. Each walk's occupation density fe on Ke is split along the decomposition He = J(He−1) ⊕ Ze used there: the part inherited from level e−1 and the genuinely new part. The new-sector energy ‖fe − J fe−1‖² is plotted relative to its i.i.d.-uniform expectation dim Ze / N, so 1 is sampling noise, values far below 1 mean a low-discrepancy walk, and values far above 1 mean new fine structure. The theorem concerns the transfer operators Le,t,x and the gap g0(p, e). Neither is computed here, so nothing in this section tests the theorem or follows from it.

    Record, ordinary and random-control words keep their own lengths (≤ 195 steps), so their fine levels are often undersampled.

    What the box is detecting

    By the Eliahou–Rozier identity (EOC Lem. 2.2), every point of a trajectory satisfies log₂ mᵢ = log₂ m₀ − Rᵢ + Eᵢ, where the carry excess Eᵢ ≥ 0 is tiny once m is large. So each path is the drift walk Ri of its word, shifted by the size of its seed. The visible differences reduce to two numbers:

    1. Endpoint depth E(D) = SN − log₂ r(D), which is where the realizer sits inside its residue class mod 2SN+1. For every synthetic class the least realizer is an ordinary member of its class, so E(D) = O(1) and the path sits at the ceiling of the box. For actual orbits the large value is automatic, as De Jesús pointed out. The seed realizes its own word and lies below 2SN+1, so r(D) = m₀ and the residual log₂(m₀/r(D)) is exactly 0, for record holders and ordinary orbits alike. Then E(D) = N·log₂3 + EN − log₂ mN exactly (Lem. 2.2), which for an orbit reaching 1 is N·log₂3 plus a carry excess of about a quarter of a bit here. Matched on length, records and ordinary orbits have the same E(D)/N. Their E(D)/SN differs only through log₂m₀/SN, which restates the record criterion: a long orbit from a small seed. So the box separates words read off a seed from words built symbolically. It does not detect an arithmetic property of record holders. The realizer frontier rmin(N, c), a minimum over all c-confined words, is not computed on this page.
    2. Shape of the drift walk. Periodic and Sturmian words are balanced (|Si − iμ| < 1); the critical ones stay inside a unit band of R, which is band rigidity (EOC App. A), and so are confined for their whole length. Actual orbits and random words wander about √N.

    Periodic vs Sturmian. On every picture-level statistic the two look the same: balanced, made of letters in {1,2}, never visiting 5 mod 8, with E(D) = O(1). The box cannot separate them. What separates them is height amortization ρN = log₂H / SN (EOC Obs. 5.9). The convergent periodic words have ρN well below 1, while every Sturmian word has ρN > 1. Arithmetically the two are linked by the 2-adic depth law in the table above: the least realizers of 1cβ and of its odd convergents agree in qn + qn+1 − 1 low bits.

    Occupation. Residue occupation is word structure again: m ≡ 5 (mod 8) exactly when d ≥ 3, so words over {1,2} never visit that class. Delay/glide record holders visit it at about 0.18, against about 0.25 for N-matched ordinary orbits and the generic 1/4. Records also have a smaller mean valuation and spend more of their steps with R ≤ 1. These differences are largely the same selection again. For an orbit that reaches 1, μ = log₂3 + (log₂m₀ + EN)/N exactly, so a small seed at fixed length forces fewer large valuations. The near-record control tests what survives once length and seed size are matched. With N matched within ±3 and the κ = log₂m₀/N trend removed, no free statistic separates records from their neighbours after a Bonferroni correction: fraction of time in the corridor, L₁, frequency of d ≥ 3, max valuation, discrepancy and max rise. With M = 8·10⁶, K = 50 and dN = 3, discrepancy (median z −0.45) and max rise (+0.20) both have p ≈ 0.05. Their signs are consistent across K ∈ {30, 50, 100} and dN ∈ {1, 3, 6}, but none reaches significance. The residual in μ is the carry-excess term EN/N, which is larger for smaller seeds. Only 9 of the 39 records have any other seed ≤ 8·10⁶ with the same (N, SN). That is why the match has to be relaxed.

    Separated returns. Records carry far more occupation than N-matched ordinary orbits, and more of it in separated returns: O₁ ≈ 64 against 9, and O₁ − ℓ₀ ≈ 17 against 2. This is the κ selection again. In the near-record control (M = 8·10⁶, K = 50, dN = 3) neither total occupation, separated-return occupation, episode count nor separated share distinguishes records from their κ-adjusted neighbours. The medians are z = −0.11, −0.29, −0.11 and −0.11, with p ≥ 0.2, against a Bonferroni threshold of 0.005 over ten free statistics. Across K ∈ {30, 50, 100} and dN ∈ {1, 3, 6} the separated part leans slightly negative, but its smallest p is 0.053. The entry lemma holds on every observed re-entry, with largest c − Ra = 0.552 < 0.585 at c = 1. The largest O₁/log₂m₀ among the record holders here is 8.41, consistent with the calibration in EOC §15. For m₀ = 285175 the decomposition is O₁ = 15 + 4 + 6 + 64 + 1 + 5 + 3 = 98, so 83 of its 98 occupation times come from separated returns.

    Residue tower (empirical Fourier diagnostic informed by the spectral-inheritance theorem). At walk length 4 000 the periodic and Sturmian walks are low-discrepancy on every sampled level: their new-sector energy is typically 10−3–10−1 of sampling noise. The two classes separate in the limit N → ∞:

    • Periodic words keep a persistent new-sector energy at a few specific levels, computed exactly in the table, and it is zero at every other level in range. Divisibility of the EOC denominator 3P − 2Q by pe is one way it arises. 13 | 35 − 28 makes w(5/8)'s walk exactly periodic mod 13 (energy 7/5), and 11 | 353 − 284 does the same for w(53/84). It is not the only way: w(12/19) carries energy at 5² and 13², although neither 5 nor 13 divides 312 − 219 = 7153.
    • Sturmian words should have no persistent energy at any level. Argument sketch: the walk's discrete log is A·⌊jλ + ρ⌋ − B·j mod |Ke|, where 2 = gA and 3 = gB. Because λ is irrational, (jλ/n mod 1, j mod n) is jointly equidistributed, which forces every nontrivial character average to 0. The convergence can be slow: the critical words show excess ≈ 24 at 7³ for N = 4 000, but the energy itself falls from 1.5 to 0.065 between N = 4 000 and 32 000. The cause of that slow level was not identified.

    Actual orbits below 106 are at most 195 steps long, so only levels with |Ke| ≤ N/4 are resolvable (e = 1, and e = 2 for p = 5, 7). There their excess (median 0.3–1.2) is indistinguishable from the random control (0.55–1.0). At these resolutions the residue tower separates periodic from Sturmian words, but it does not separate actual orbits from generic words.
