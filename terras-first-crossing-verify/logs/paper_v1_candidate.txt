                                                Abstract
          Terras’s coefficient stopping time conjecture asks whether the shortcut Collatz orbit of
      every integer n ≥ 2 lies below n when its coefficient ρk = 3d /2k first falls below one. Here
      k counts shortcut steps and d counts their odd inputs. We prove that every eligible realizer
      descends when the first-crossing word has at most 75 maximal runs of odd inputs. Any
      counterexample requires at least 76 such runs, 10 439 860 591 steps and 6 586 818 670 odd
      inputs. The proof uses a lower bound on the seed from the length of its odd runs, rational
      approximations to log2 3, the verified range of Rozier and Terracol, and Rhin’s estimate
      for a linear form in logarithms. We also prove descent for a family with arbitrarily many
      odd runs. Two further restrictions narrow the remaining words. An initial square U U in
      a failing word satisfies |U | < 35 log2 k. For a counterexample of shortest crossing length,
      the reciprocal-sum estimate of Nikzad, Frinkle and Bagherzadeh gives a lower bound on
      ρk whose value begins 0.9441733669 . . .. The open inequality compares the least eligible
      starting value in each remaining parity class with an explicit threshold.


1    What is being asked
Write T (n) = (3n + 1)/2 at odd inputs and T (n) = n/2 at even inputs. Fix n ≥ 2. For
the parity word w of its first k input states n, T (n), . . . , T k−1 (n), let d count the odd inputs.
Induction gives
                                     2k T k (n) = 3d n + Cw ,                                      (1)
where the nonnegative integer Cw depends only on w. Write ρk = 3d /2k with ρ0 = 1. An odd
step multiplies the coefficient by 3/2 and an even step halves it. Let τ (n) be the first k with
ρk < 1, and let χ(n) be the first k with T k (n) < n. An empty defining set gives stopping time
∞. Equation (1) gives τ (n) ≤ χ(n), so any failure of Terras’s conjecture τ (n) = χ(n) has finite
k = τ (n) and T k (n) ≥ n [10, 2]. An even starting value descends at its first step, so a failure
has odd n and d ≥ 1.
    A first-crossing word has ρj ≥ 1 for every j < k and ρk < 1. Its last step is even, so
ρk ∈ [1/2, 1). With d ≥ 1, this also gives 2k−1 ≤ 3d < 2k and k = ⌈d log2 3⌉ ≤ 2d. An odd run
is a maximal block of consecutive odd input states.

Remark 2 (Testing all eligible realizers of a word). A length-k parity word specifies one residue
class modulo 2k . Indeed, the two lifts of a given length-j class modulo 2j+1 have starting values
that differ by 2j . Their images after j steps differ by 3dj , an odd integer, so exactly one lift
has the prescribed next parity. For a first-crossing word, let r(w) be the least integer ≥ 2 in
its class. Every eligible realizer descends if and only if r(w)(ρ−1k − 1) > Mw , since the left side
increases with the seed. If a seed n fails, its endpoint satisfies 0 ≤ T k (n) − n < s by Proposition
1 and (3).

Theorem 5. Every eligible realizer of a first-crossing word with at most 75 odd runs descends

time k ≥ 10 439 860 591 and odd-input count d ≥ 6 586 818 670.

Remark 6 (Dependencies of Theorem 5). The Rozier–Terracol verification supplies the seed
bound n > B. Combined with (4), the Farey interval then gives the two integer bounds. In
the proof of Theorem 5, Rhin’s estimate enters only in the case d ≥ 261 . Rozier and Terracol
[9, Rem. 5.5] credit Simons with proposing the classification by local maxima. Priority for the
stated first-crossing bounds remains open.


4    A family with arbitrarily many runs
Proposition 7. Repeat the block 110 exactly h ≥ 1 times and append zeros up to the first
coefficient crossing. Every eligible realizer descends at that crossing.
Proof. One block acts by H(x) + 5 = 9(x + 5)/8, so −5 is its fixed point. The block’s full
coefficient is 9/8 and its two nonempty proper-prefix coefficients are 3/2 and 9/4. Consequently
the first crossing occurs among the appended zeros at k = ⌈h log2 9⌉ ≤ 4h. Iteration gives
H h (n) + 5 = 9h (n + 5)/8h . Integrality implies 8h | n + 5, hence n ≥ 8h − 5. The normalized
memory is 5(1 − (8/9)h ) < 5 and the appended zeros leave it unchanged.
    For h ≤ 63, k ≤ 252 is below the crossing-length bound in Theorem 5. For h ≥ 64, Rhin’s
estimate gives nΛ ≥ (8h − 5)/(4h)13.3 > 5. Indeed, 864 − 5 > 5 · 25614 , and ((h + 1)/h)14 ≤
(65/64)14 < 8, so 8h − 5 > 5(4h)14 persists by induction. A failure requires nΛ < n(eΛ − 1) ≤
Mw < 5, a contradiction.

   The prefix (110)h and the completed word generally have different least seeds. Write m =
k − 3h and n = 8h t − 5. All these n form the binary prefix class, since 23h = 8h is its exact
modulus. Its least positive representative is 8h −5. To append m zeros we also need 2m | 9h t−5,
the block endpoint. At h = 12 this gives k = 39, m = 3 and t ≡ 5 (mod 8). The prefix and
completed-word least seeds are therefore 68 719 476 731 and 343 597 383 675, respectively.

Proposition 8. Let a first-crossing word from n begin with U U , where |U | = ℓ ≥ 1. Then
(4/3)ℓ < n + 1. If the crossing fails to give descent, then

                              log2 (n + 1)      14.3
                         ℓ<                <            log2 k < 35 log2 k.
                               log2 (4/3)    log2 (4/3)

Proof. Set x = T ℓ (n). Both n and x start with U , so the parity-class congruence gives x ≡ n
(mod 2ℓ ). Since ℓ < k, ρℓ ≥ 1. Equality would give 3dℓ = 2ℓ , impossible for ℓ ≥ 1. Thus
x ≥ ρℓ n > n, so x − n is a positive multiple of 2ℓ . Equivalently 2ℓ (x − n) is a positive multiple
of 22ℓ . At each step T (y) + 1 ≤ (3/2)(y + 1), whence

                               2ℓ ≤ x − n < x + 1 ≤ (3/2)ℓ (n + 1).

Dividing gives the first assertion, independently of whether the seed descends.
    For a failure set Λ = k ln 2 − d ln 3 > 0. Then nΛ < n(eΛ − 1) ≤ Mw ≤ d/3 by Proposition 1
and (3). Since k ≥ 2 and d ≤ k, Rhin’s bound [9, Prop. 6.3] gives Λ ≥ k −13.3 . It follows that
n < (d/3)k 13.3 ≤ k 14.3 /3 and hence n + 1 < k 14.3 . Taking logarithms completes the proof, since
14.3/ log2 (4/3) < 35.

   The restriction concerns repetition inside a single finite first-crossing prefix. The periodic-
approximation results in [3] concern infinite parity words.

7    The open question
A shortest counterexample would have at least 76 odd runs, obey the coefficient bound in
Proposition 9, and have no initial square whose period exceeds the bound in Proposition 8.
For each remaining first-crossing word w, its coefficient and its additive correction are known
exactly. The open estimate for its least eligible realizer r(w) is

                                              Cw       Mw
                                   r(w) >           = −1    .
                                            2k − 3d  ρk − 1

Equality already gives a counterexample. A lower bound for r(w) or an upper bound for the
right side can certify a word if it preserves this strict inequality. The rational-shadow estimates
of [4] supply bounds for individual blocks. Applying one to this question requires transporting
it back to the starting seed and comparing it with the threshold of the actual first-crossing
prefix.

Reproducibility
The companion verifier checks the numerical inequalities used in the proofs, recomputes the
finite bracket count, and verifies the worked examples using integer and rational arithmetic. It
lives in the companion repository https://github.com/innerlightr-wq/terras-first-crossing,
with the verification code pinned at the immutable commit a93b1e8 (full hash a93b1e82d13fab7024c380ae3632
Run the following command from the project root.
python3 scripts/verify_crossing.py
The script checks the numerical inequalities with integers and rationals, encloses ln 2 and ln 3
by a 96-term series with a geometric tail, recomputes the finite bracket sum in (164) of [7],
and encloses each exponential cutoff by alternating Taylor bounds. The SHA-256 digest of the
verifier in this review copy begins f852c88311071a0d. The orbit and residue-class examples are
finite checks. The proofs for arbitrary word lengths are in the text.
    The finite seed verification of Rozier–Terracol, Rhin’s estimate, the high-path accounting
of Nikzad–Frinkle–Bagherzadeh and the height record cited above are external inputs. The
general case of Terras’s conjecture remains open.
